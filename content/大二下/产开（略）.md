### What is an opportunity

- an idea for a new product
- It is a product description in an embryonic form
  - a newly sensed need
  - a newly discovered technology
  - a rough match between a need and a possible solution
- It can be thought of as a hypothesis about how value might be created.
### Ansoff’s growth matrix

  ![image](https://api2.mubu.com/v3/document_image/23450990_b44b803b-f302-41d8-85ff-b545b0ad9ebf.png)
- 1.market penetration
- 2.market development
- 3.product development
- 4.diversification
- Ansoff’s growth matrix and risk
  - The element of risk increases the further the strategy moves away from known quantities - the existing product and the existing market
  - Product development (requiring, in effect, a new product) and market extension (a new market) involve a greater risk than market penetration
  - Diversification (both new products and new markets) generally carries the greatest risk of all
### Opportunity identification process

- stablish a charter
- Generate & sense many opportunities
- Screen opportunities
- Develop promising opportunities
- Select exceptional opportunities
  - Real-Win-Worth-it（It summarises 3 questions that the organisation should attempt to answer when looking for the exceptional opportunities）
    - Is the opportunity real?
    - Can you win with this opportunity?
    - Is the opportunity worth it financially?
- Reflect on the results and the process

### Design for Manufacturing (DFM) the 5-step process

![image](https://api2.mubu.com/v3/document_image/23450990_d2483276-d453-4330-e2af-943550848eb2.png)

- 1 Understanding manufacturing cost
  - Component costs
    - standard parts
    - custom components
  - Assembly costs
  - Overhead costs
    - Support costs
    - Indirect allocations
- 2 Reduce the costs of components
  - Understand the process constraints and cost drivers
  - Redesign components to eliminate processing steps
  - Choose the appropriate economic scale for the part process
  - Standardise components and processes
- 3 Reduce the costs of assembly
- 4 Reduce the costs of supporting production
  - Key ideas of DFA

    - Maximise the ease of inserting parts
  - Benefits of DFA

### Definition

- Intellectual property, often known as IP, refers to intangible assets created by human intellectual or creative activity.
- Intellectual property rights (IPRs) allow people to own their creativity and innovation in the same way that they can own physical property.

### The four main types of IP

- patents for inventions ：new and improved products and processes that are capable of industrial application
- trademarks for brand identity：of goods and services, allowing distinctions to be made between different traders .
- designs for product appearance
- copyright for original expression
- Trade secrets
